# Product

## Register

brand

## Platform

web

## Users

Prospective home buyers evaluating a boutique, upper-segment residential developer. Typically families or established professionals making one of the largest purchases of their lives. They are unhurried and skeptical: they return to the site several times over weeks, compare the firm against others, and look for reasons to trust (or distrust) the developer before ever calling. Post-2023-earthquake, structural safety and the developer's track record weigh heavily on the decision. Most visits happen on mobile, often shared between family members.

Secondary users: the firm's own staff, editing all content through Sanity Studio without developer help.

## Product Purpose

A marketing site for a fictional boutique residential developer (3–8 projects, a single city, family-run, likely second generation). It is built as a strong, coherent demo to be resold to real developers by re-skinning tokens (color, type, logo) and replacing Sanity content, not by diluting the design.

The site exists to move a buyer from curiosity to contacting the sales office (call, WhatsApp, or appointment request). Trust is the supporting layer that makes that contact happen: a visible delivery record, technical transparency (ground survey, concrete class, building inspection firm, occupancy permit dates), and the people behind the firm.

Success: a visitor can understand a project, believe the firm will deliver it, and reach a named person at the sales office within a couple of taps.

### Content policy (decided)

- **No prices.** Prices are never shown; where a price would appear, the site shows the sales office's CTA text (from Sanity) and routes to a call. Rationale: upper-segment convention, and it drives the primary action. Outdated prices in an inflationary market would also erode trust.
- **One sales office, presented as a person.** A single named contact (name, role, photo, phone, WhatsApp, hours) lives in Site Settings and is used across every project. Not per-project representatives.
- **Actual delivery date is optional.** The planned-vs-actual delivery comparison is shown only when the firm fills in the actual date. A firm with late deliveries can omit it without the design showing a gap. This is the first objection a real client will raise; the design must never force it.
- **Empty fields are invisible.** Projects in "Yakında" status will have half-empty spec sheets. Missing data is never rendered as "—", "N/A", or placeholders; the row simply doesn't exist.

## Brand Personality

Rooted, personal, candid. A local family firm that has built in this district for decades and puts its name on every building. Warm but not sentimental; confident but never boastful. It proves rather than claims: dates, documents, and finished buildings instead of counters and superlatives. The emotional goal is calm confidence: "these people will still be here after the keys are handed over."

## Brand Identity (demo firm)

Fictional; replaced per client. Checked 2026-09-28: no known firm named exactly "Tınaz Yapı" (a differently spelled "Tanaz Yapı" exists; mind the domain if one is bought for the demo).

- **Name:** Tınaz Yapı. Uppercase is "TINAZ YAPI" (Turkish ı → I, no dot).
- **Tagline:** "Adımızı kapıya yazıyoruz."
- **City:** Bakırköy, İstanbul (one district: Kartaltepe, Cevizlik, Zeytinlik, Osmaniye, Sakızağacı, Şenlikköy, Yeşilköy). Changed from Urla on 2026-09-28. The core business is urban renewal (kentsel dönüşüm) of 1960s–70s apartment parcels: 4–8 storey apartment buildings of 8–30 homes. No towers. Much of coastal Bakırköy sits on soft alluvial ground (ZC/ZD), so openly published ground class and foundation data is the firm's strongest proof.
- **Origin:** Founded in 1981 by **Hasan Tınaz**, a kalfa (master builder) who put up his first apartment building in Bakırköy that year and stayed in the district. Today the firm is run by his daughter **Elif Tınaz**, a civil engineer, who rebuilds the neighbourhood's aging stock under urban renewal. The story is the handover from master builder to engineer: handwork plus engineering transparency.
- **Sales office contact:** **Deniz Aksoy** (the named person in Site Settings).
- **Where the founding year lives:** inside the story on the About page, never as an "est. 1981" badge, logo line, or eyebrow.

## Anti-references

- The classic Turkish contractor site: full-screen slider, three icon service cards, "25+ Years / 5000+ Homes" counters, orange-and-navy palette.
- Luxury real-estate gloss: black and gold, script fonts, "prestige" and "privilege" copy, drone-video hero.
- Cold corporate holding: gray-blue, stock handshake photos, investor-relations tone.
- SaaS / startup landing: gradients, glassmorphism, heavily rounded cards, "Get started" energy.
- The AI reflex for "boutique family firm": cream background, italic display serif, "est. 19xx" eyebrows, editorial-magazine layout.

## Design Principles

1. **Proof over claims.** Every trust statement is backed by something checkable: a delivery date, a document, a finished building, a named person. No counters, no superlatives.
2. **Warmth comes from people and place.** Photography, material, and plain-spoken copy carry the warmth; the color system does not fake it.
3. **Unhurried pacing.** The buyer is deliberating, not impulse-buying. Give depth and room; no pressure tactics, pop-ups, or countdowns.
4. **The sales office is always one step away.** Contact is persistent and human (a named person, a real phone number), never a buried form.
5. **One firm, one voice.** Commit to a distinctive identity. Resale happens through tokens and content, never by making the design generic.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA. Body text ≥4.5:1 contrast, including over photography. All motion has a `prefers-reduced-motion` alternative. Older buyers are a core audience: generous base font size, large tap targets, no information conveyed by color alone. Turkish-language content throughout; layouts must tolerate long Turkish compound words.
